How to use these plans

  • These 10 plans are written for Class 3. Change the numbers, contexts and difficulty level for Classes 1 to 5.
  • Each lesson moves from concrete materials to drawings and then to numbers and symbols, so children understand an idea before they use a rule.
  • The materials are low-cost: sticks, straws, bottle caps, seeds, paper and chalk. Use whatever your school and community offer.
  • Each plan has space for the curricular goal and competency number. Fill it in from your textbook or curriculum.
  • Children may explain their thinking in the language they are most comfortable with. Introduce mathematical words slowly and use them often.
  • The assessment section of each plan points out common errors. Use them to plan the next lesson, as the guide asks for error analysis.
  • Your supervisor will assess five lessons in each subject. Keep all 10 plans ready in the file and fill in the reflection after every lesson.

Lesson plan 1

Hundreds, Tens and Ones

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandNumber sense and place valueDuration40 minutes
TopicGrouping into tens and hundreds and building 3-digit numbers
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children develop number sense by seeing how numbers are built from hundreds, tens and ones.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Group objects into tens and make a hundred from ten tens.
  • Show a 3-digit number using bundles, drawings and a place-value mat.
  • Tell the value of each digit, for example the 4 in 347 stands for 40.

Teaching-learning materials

  • About 300 ice-cream sticks or straws with rubber bands
  • Place-value mats (Hundreds | Tens | Ones) drawn on paper
  • Number cards 0 to 9
  • Chalk and board

Prior knowledge

Children can count up to 100 and know tens and ones in 2-digit numbers.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchShow a large pile of sticks and ask, “How can we count them quickly?” Let children suggest ways and lead them to grouping in tens.5 minutes
ConcreteGroups bundle sticks into tens, then tie ten tens into one hundred. They place bundles on the mat to make numbers such as 134: one hundred bundle, three ten bundles and four loose sticks.12 minutes
PictorialThe teacher draws a square for a hundred, a line for a ten and a dot for a one. Children draw 253 in this way in their notebooks.8 minutes
SymbolicWrite 253 and its expanded form, 200 + 50 + 3. Ask, “Which digit is in the tens place? What is its value?” Repeat with other numbers.8 minutes
Practice and reflectNumber-maker game: each child draws three digit cards, makes a number, builds or draws it and says it aloud. Close by asking, “Why do we make bundles of ten?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Observe: Can the child bundle correctly? Can the child build a given number? Can the child tell the value of each digit?
  • Note common errors, such as reading 305 as “three hundred fifty”, and revisit zero as a place holder.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Give larger objects to children who find small sticks hard to handle.
  • Let children explain their thinking in the language they are comfortable with.
  • Challenge advanced children with numbers that contain zero, such as 306 and 450.

Extension / homework

Collect 30 small things at home (seeds, pebbles, caps), group them in tens and tell how many tens and ones you have.

Lesson plan 2

Which Number Is Bigger?

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandNumber sense and place valueDuration40 minutes
TopicComparing and ordering 3-digit numbers
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children compare and order numbers using place value and a number line.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Compare two 3-digit numbers by looking first at hundreds, then tens, then ones.
  • Use the signs >, < and = correctly.
  • Arrange four numbers from smallest to biggest and place them on a number line.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Place-value mats and bundles of sticks
  • Number cards (3-digit numbers)
  • A number line from 0 to 1000 drawn with chalk on the floor
  • Cards with the signs >, < and =

Prior knowledge

Children can build and read 3-digit numbers using hundreds, tens and ones.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchShow two bags with different numbers of sticks and ask, “Which has more? How do you know?”5 minutes
ConcretePairs build 245 and 254 on mats using bundles. They compare hundreds first, then tens, then ones, and explain which number is greater and why.10 minutes
PictorialChildren stand as number cards on the floor number line. The class decides who should stand to the left and right of each other.8 minutes
SymbolicIntroduce the signs: the open mouth of the sign faces the bigger number. Write 245 < 254 and 387 > 378. Order four number cards from smallest to biggest.9 minutes
Practice and reflectNumber race: each group orders a set of number cards in the fewest seconds. Ask, “What did you check first?”8 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child compare numbers and give a reason using place value?
  • Does the child use the signs correctly?
  • Note children who compare digits from the ones place instead of the hundreds place.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Colour hundreds, tens and ones digits differently on cards for children who mix up places.
  • Ask advanced children to make the greatest and smallest number from three digit cards.

Extension / homework

Write the house numbers or prices you see at home and arrange them from smallest to biggest.

Lesson plan 3

Adding with Regrouping

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandOperations (addition)Duration40 minutes
TopicAddition of 2- and 3-digit numbers with carrying
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand addition with regrouping and use it to solve everyday problems.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Add two numbers using bundles and trade ten ones for one ten.
  • Record addition in the vertical form with a carry.
  • Check whether an answer is reasonable by estimating first.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Bundles of sticks, loose sticks and place-value mats
  • Story problem cards
  • Chart paper with the vertical addition format

Prior knowledge

Children can add 2-digit numbers without regrouping and know the place value of digits.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchTell a story: Meena has 47 marbles and Rohan gives her 38 more. Ask for a guess about how many she has now (about 80?).5 minutes
ConcretePairs show 47 and 38 with bundles. They add the ones: 7 + 8 = 15, trade ten loose sticks for one new bundle, and then count the tens to get 85.12 minutes
PictorialThe teacher shows the same problem as drawings and writes it in the vertical form, showing a small 1 carried into the tens column.8 minutes
SymbolicSolve 258 + 167 together (answer 425), then more examples in pairs. Children compare their answer with their first guess.8 minutes
Practice and reflectPairs solve story problems from cards. Ask, “When do we need to carry? Why?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child explain what happens to the extra ten?
  • Look for common errors: forgetting the carry or writing 47 + 38 = 715 by adding digits separately.
  • Ask the child to estimate before solving.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children who need support keep using bundles until they feel confident.
  • Ask advanced children to write their own addition story and swap with a partner.

Extension / homework

Ask a family member for two prices you can add together, and show how you added them.

Lesson plan 4

Subtracting with Regrouping

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandOperations (subtraction)Duration40 minutes
TopicSubtraction of 2- and 3-digit numbers with borrowing
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand subtraction with regrouping and apply it to everyday situations.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Subtract using bundles by breaking one ten into ten ones.
  • Record the subtraction in vertical form with regrouping.
  • Check a subtraction answer using addition.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Bundles of sticks, loose sticks and place-value mats
  • Story problem cards
  • Chart with the vertical subtraction format

Prior knowledge

Children can subtract without regrouping and add with regrouping.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchStory: A shopkeeper has 62 bananas and sells 28. Ask, “About how many are left?” Let children guess.5 minutes
ConcreteShow 62 as six bundles and two sticks. There are not enough ones to take away 8, so children break one bundle into ten sticks, take away 28 and count what is left: 34.12 minutes
PictorialDraw the problem. Cross out the part taken away. Write it in vertical form and mark the regrouping.8 minutes
SymbolicSolve 341 − 157 together (answer 184). Check by adding 184 + 157 to get 341.8 minutes
Practice and reflectPairs solve story problems. Ask, “Why can’t we do 2 − 8 in the ones place? What did we do instead?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Look for the common error of subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger digit, for example 62 − 28 = 46.
  • Can the child explain why one ten is broken?
  • Can the child check the answer using addition?

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use real objects like bottle caps for children who need more hands-on work.
  • Ask advanced children to write a problem where the answer is 100.

Extension / homework

Find out how many days are left until your next birthday or festival and explain how you worked it out.

Lesson plan 5

Equal Groups: Multiplication

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandOperations (multiplication)Duration40 minutes
TopicMultiplication as equal groups, repeated addition and arrays; tables of 2, 5 and 10
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand multiplication as combining equal groups and begin to build multiplication facts.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Show multiplication with equal groups and as repeated addition.
  • Write multiplication sentences such as 3 × 4 = 12.
  • Use skip counting and arrays to build the tables of 2, 5 and 10.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Paper plates and counters (seeds or bottle caps)
  • Grid paper or an array chart
  • A 1 to 100 number chart
  • Story cards

Prior knowledge

Children can add equal numbers and count in 2s, 5s and 10s.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchAsk: “There are 3 plates and each plate has 4 laddoos. How many laddoos in all?” Let children find the answer in their own way.5 minutes
ConcreteGroups make 3 plates with 4 counters each and write 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. They try other examples: 5 plates with 2 counters, 4 plates with 5 counters.12 minutes
PictorialDraw equal groups and rows of dots (arrays). Show that 3 rows of 4 gives the same total as 4 rows of 3.8 minutes
SymbolicWrite 3 × 4 = 12 and read it as “3 groups of 4”. Colour multiples of 2, 5 and 10 on the number chart and notice the patterns.8 minutes
Practice and reflectChildren solve story problems about equal groups. Ask, “How is multiplication different from adding any numbers?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child show a given multiplication with groups or arrays?
  • Does the child connect repeated addition with the multiplication sentence?
  • Note whether the child can skip count by 2, 5 and 10.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Give extra counters and a number chart to children who need support.
  • Challenge advanced children to build the table of 3 or 4 using arrays.

Extension / homework

Find things at home that come in equal groups (eggs in a tray, fingers on hands) and write multiplication sentences for them.

Lesson plan 6

Sharing Equally: Division

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandOperations (division)Duration40 minutes
TopicDivision as equal sharing and equal grouping
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand division as fair sharing and grouping and link it with multiplication.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Share objects equally among a given number of groups.
  • Make equal groups of a given size and tell how many groups there are.
  • Write division sentences such as 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and link them with multiplication.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Counters and paper plates
  • Story cards
  • Chart showing fact families

Prior knowledge

Children understand multiplication as equal groups and know some multiplication facts.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchAsk: “12 laddoos are shared equally among 3 children. How many does each child get?” Children suggest ways to share.5 minutes
ConcreteSharing: groups place 12 counters on 3 plates, one at a time, until none are left. Grouping: they make groups of 3 from 12 counters and count the groups.12 minutes
PictorialDraw plates and dots to show both ways. Discuss how both give 4.8 minutes
SymbolicWrite 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Show the fact family: 3 × 4 = 12, 4 × 3 = 12, 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 12 ÷ 4 = 3.8 minutes
Practice and reflectSolve sharing stories in pairs. Discuss what happens when we share 13 among 3 (one is left over). Ask, “How can multiplication help us divide?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child share equally and explain what “equal” means?
  • Can the child write the division sentence for a story?
  • Does the child use a multiplication fact to check a division?

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use real objects like rotis, sweets or bottle caps for sharing.
  • Ask advanced children to write their own division story with a leftover.

Extension / homework

Share a group of 12 items at home among family members and tell how many each gets.

Lesson plan 7

Half and Quarter

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandFractionsDuration40 minutes
TopicEqual parts, one-half and one-quarter using paper folding
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand a fraction as one of several equal parts of a whole.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Fold shapes into two and four equal parts.
  • Name and write one-half (1/2) and one-quarter (1/4).
  • Tell whether a shape has been divided into equal parts.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Paper circles and squares
  • Pictures of shapes divided into equal and unequal parts
  • Colour pencils
  • A rope and 8 counters

Prior knowledge

Children can share things equally and understand the idea of “fair share”.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchAsk: “One roti is to be shared fairly between two children. How should we cut it?” Discuss why the parts must be equal.5 minutes
ConcreteEach child folds a paper circle into two equal parts and then into four. They check by placing the parts on top of each other. They colour one part in each case.12 minutes
PictorialShow shapes divided in different ways. Children decide which are divided into equal parts and shade one-half or one-quarter.8 minutes
SymbolicWrite 1/2 and 1/4 and read them as “one-half” and “one-quarter”. Talk about what the top and bottom numbers tell us.7 minutes
Practice and reflectFraction hunt: find halves and quarters in the room (window panes, tiles). Find half of 8 counters and a quarter of 8 counters.8 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child fold a shape into equal parts?
  • Does the child reject unequal parts?
  • Does the child connect 1/2 and 1/4 to the right pictures?

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use thick paper or cloth for children who find paper folding difficult.
  • Challenge advanced children to show 3/4 of a shape.

Extension / homework

Fold a piece of paper into quarters at home and use it to show your family what one-quarter means.

Lesson plan 8

How Long Is It?

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandMeasurementDuration40 minutes
TopicMeasuring length with non-standard units and then a ruler in centimetres
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand why we need standard units and measure lengths using a ruler.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Measure a length using non-standard units like a hand span or a pencil.
  • Explain why measurements in hand spans differ from child to child.
  • Use a ruler to measure objects in centimetres, starting from zero.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Rulers (cm)
  • Strings and strips of paper
  • Classroom objects of different lengths
  • A metre stick or tape

Prior knowledge

Children understand the words long, short, longer and shorter.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchAsk three children to measure the length of the desk using their hand spans. The answers are different. Ask, “Why?”5 minutes
ConcreteGroups measure objects using a pencil, a hand span and a string, and compare their results. The class decides that we need the same unit for everyone.12 minutes
PictorialShow a ruler on the board. Point out zero, the centimetre marks and where to start. Children draw a line of 8 cm.8 minutes
SymbolicRecord measurements like “The pencil is 15 cm long.” Introduce the metre stick and compare it with the ruler.8 minutes
Practice and reflectMeasurement hunt: estimate and then measure five objects and record them in a table. Ask, “How close was your guess?”7 minutes

Assessment

  • Does the child start measuring at zero?
  • Are estimates close to the actual measurements?
  • Does the child write the unit with the number?

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use tactile rulers or strips with raised marks for children with low vision.
  • Challenge advanced children to find two objects whose lengths add up to 30 cm.

Extension / homework

Measure three things at home with your hand span and then with a ruler, and tell which is easier to share with others.

Our Favourite Fruit (Pictograph)

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandData handlingDuration40 minutes
TopicCollecting data, making a pictograph and reading it
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children collect and organise information and use it to answer questions.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Collect data by asking classmates and recording with tally marks.
  • Make a pictograph in which one picture stands for one child.
  • Read the pictograph and answer questions about most, least and how many more.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Pictures of four fruits (mango, banana, apple, guava)
  • Sticky notes or paper squares
  • Chart paper and markers

Prior knowledge

Children can count objects and compare numbers.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
LaunchAsk: “Which fruit do you like the most?” Introduce the four choices. Tell the class we will find out which fruit is the class favourite.5 minutes
Collecting dataEach child sticks a note under the picture of their favourite fruit. The teacher also records the votes with tally marks on the board.10 minutes
Making the pictographOn chart paper, children draw one picture for each child. They agree on a key: 1 picture = 1 child. They label the chart.10 minutes
Reading the dataAsk: Which fruit is liked most? Which is liked least? How many more children like mango than banana? How many children are there in all?10 minutes
ReflectAsk: “What else could we find out by collecting information?” Display the chart in the classroom.5 minutes

Assessment

  • Does the child record the votes correctly?
  • Can the child read the pictograph and answer questions?
  • Can the child explain what the key tells us?

Inclusion and extra support

  • Pair children who need support with a partner when they draw the chart.
  • Challenge advanced children to make a pictograph in which 1 picture stands for 2 children.

Extension / homework

Ask five family members or neighbours their favourite fruit and show the answers in a small pictograph.

Lesson plan 10

Assessment and Review (Three Stations)

ClassIII (Class 3)SubjectMathematics
StrandAssessmentDuration40 minutes
TopicCombined check of number sense, operations, problem solving and communication, with self-correction
Date Teacher 
Goal / competency no.(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Understand each child’s progress in mathematics in a playful way and plan the next steps for learning.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Build a number with materials and tell the value of its digits.
  • Complete a worksheet on numbers, operations and fractions.
  • Solve a story problem and explain how they got the answer.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Bundles of sticks and place-value mats
  • Worksheet and story problem cards
  • Observation checklist
  • Star or smiley stickers

Prior knowledge

The practice from the previous nine lessons.

Teaching process

StageTeaching activityTime
InstructionsExplain the three stations and divide the class into three groups. Tell the children this is not a test, just a way to see how they are learning. Each group visits every station in turn.4 minutes
Station 1: Show and tellEach child builds a number the teacher names (for example, 346) with bundles and tells the value of each digit. The teacher fills in the observation checklist.8 minutes
Station 2: WorksheetChildren complete the worksheet (sample below).8 minutes
Station 3: Problem solvingPairs solve a story problem and explain their method with drawings or materials. The teacher listens to their reasoning.8 minutes
Self-correctionPartners check each other’s worksheets against a model answer. Children correct their own mistakes and give themselves a star or smiley.8 minutes
Wrap-upThe teacher gives general feedback and tells the class what they will practise next.4 minutes

Sample worksheet questions

A. Write the number: 4 hundreds, 2 tens and 7 ones.

B. Put >, < or = in the box: 345 [ ] 354.

C. Add: 126 + 137 = ___

D. Subtract: 81 − 36 = ___

E. Fill in: 4 × 3 = ___ and 12 ÷ 4 = ___

F. Draw a line in the square to show one-half.

G. There are 3 plates with 5 oranges on each plate. How many oranges are there in all? Show how you found the answer.

Assessment

  • Fill in the table below for each child in all four areas.
  • Choose the areas where most children are at “Still learning” and plan extra practice for them.
  • Note common errors and use them in the next class.
AreaStill learning (1)Getting there (2)Confident (3)
Number sense and place valueCounts objects but needs help to group in tens and hundredsBuilds and reads 3-digit numbers with materialsReads, writes, compares and explains the value of each digit
OperationsNeeds concrete materials for every stepDoes addition, subtraction and multiplication facts with occasional errorsChooses the right operation and solves correctly, with regrouping
Problem solving and reasoningNeeds the problem read and explainedSolves one-step problems and gives an answerSolves the problem and explains why the answer makes sense
Mathematical communicationShows the answer without explainingExplains with materials or a drawingExplains clearly in words, drawings and symbols

Inclusion and extra support

  • Allow children who find writing hard to answer orally or with materials.
  • Give all children enough time and look at each child’s own progress, not comparison with others.

Extension / homework

Tell someone at home one thing you can do well in mathematics and one thing you want to practise.
