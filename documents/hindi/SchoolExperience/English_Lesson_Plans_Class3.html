How to use these plans

  • These 10 plans are written for Class 3. Change the words, texts and difficulty level for Classes 1 to 5.
  • The reading and writing lessons include short original texts, so the plans work with any textbook. Replace them with a lesson from your own textbook if you prefer.
  • Each plan is 40 minutes. Adjust the timings to match your period.
  • Each plan has space for the curricular goal and competency number. Fill it in from your textbook or curriculum.
  • In Language-II, the home language is a resource. Let children say or explain things first in their home language, then help them say it in English.
  • Your supervisor will assess five lessons in each subject. Keep all 10 plans ready in the file and fill in the reflection after every lesson.

Lesson plan 1

Hello, This Is Me

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Oral language (listening and speaking)

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Greetings, self-introduction and simple questions

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children listen to and use simple spoken English in everyday situations, building on what they already know in their home language.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Greet others and respond, using “Good morning. How are you? I am fine.”
  • Introduce themselves in three sentences: “My name is … I am … years old. I live in …”
  • Ask a friend, “What is your name?” and listen to the answer.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Name badges or name cards
  • A hand puppet
  • Picture cards for morning, afternoon and evening
  • Chart with sentence frames

Prior knowledge

Children know a few English words such as hello and bye, and can introduce themselves in their home language.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Warm-up

The teacher greets each child at the door with the puppet. Children reply in any language and the teacher repeats the greeting in English.

5 minutes

Teaching greetings

The teacher and the puppet act out a short greeting dialogue. Children repeat in chorus, then row by row. Picture cards show which greeting fits morning, afternoon and evening.

8 minutes

Pair practice

Children walk around, greet a classmate and ask, “Hello! What is your name?” They answer, “My name is …” using the chart.

10 minutes

Self-introduction

In a circle, each child says three sentences using the frame. The teacher helps with words. The class listens and claps.

10 minutes

Wrap-up

The class says the greetings together. One child introduces the puppet to the class.

7 minutes

Assessment

  • Observation checklist: responds to a greeting, uses the sentence frame, pronunciation, willingness to speak.
  • Record who needs more practice and give them a turn in the next lesson.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children answer first in their home language, then help them say it in English.
  • Pair shy children with a friendly partner.
  • For children with hearing difficulty, use gestures and picture cards along with speech.

Extension / homework

Greet one family member in English this evening and tell the class what they said.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson plan 2

The Lion and the Mouse

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Oral language (storytelling)

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Listening to a story, answering questions and retelling in sequence

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children listen to a story, follow what happens and retell it in simple English.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Arrange the events of the story in the right order.
  • Answer who, where, what and why questions in words or short sentences.
  • Retell the story using picture cards and the words “first, then, at last”.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Five picture cards: lion sleeping, mouse running over the lion, lion catching the mouse, lion caught in the net, mouse cutting the net
  • Rope or string for the net
  • Simple masks or puppets

Prior knowledge

Children know the names of common animals in English (lion, mouse) or can say them in their home language.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Before the story

Show pictures of the lion and the mouse. Teach the words lion, mouse, net and forest. Ask children to guess what the story might be about.

6 minutes

Telling the story

The teacher tells the story with actions and expressive voice, showing the pictures. Repeated phrases are said together. The teacher pauses to ask, “What will the mouse do now?”

10 minutes

Talking about the story

Ask: Who was sleeping? Where was the lion? What happened to the lion? Why did the lion let the mouse go? Why did the mouse help the lion?

8 minutes

Retelling

Groups put the picture cards in order and retell the story using “First … Then … At last …”. The teacher helps with words.

10 minutes

Wrap-up

Groups act out the story. The class says what the story teaches: small friends can help big friends.

6 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child put the events in order?
  • Note whether answers come as single words, short phrases or full sentences.
  • Note use of “first, then, at last”.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Allow children to answer in their home language first, then repeat in English.
  • Give children who find speaking hard a card to hold and point to while the group retells.
  • Ask advanced children to think of a different ending.

Extension / homework

Tell the story at home in your own language or in English, and draw your favourite part.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Clap and Stamp (Rhyme and Action Words)

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Oral language (rhymes and songs)

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Action words, rhythm and rhyming words

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children enjoy English rhymes and songs and begin to notice rhythm, repeated sounds and action words.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Say and act out a four-line rhyme.
  • Understand and use action words: clap, stamp, turn, touch, sit.
  • Identify rhyming words such as feet and seat, and add a new verse.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Rhyme written on a chart
  • Action word cards with pictures
  • Rhyming word cards: feet–seat, cat–hat, bat–mat

Prior knowledge

Children have played action games and know a few English words for body parts.

Rhyme (written by the teacher)

Clap, clap, clap your hands,

Stamp, stamp, stamp your feet,

Turn around and touch the ground,

Then sit down on your seat.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Warm-up

Play a simple “Teacher says” game with the action words: clap, stamp, turn, sit. Children respond with actions.

6 minutes

Learning the rhyme

The teacher says the rhyme with actions. Children repeat two lines at a time, then the whole rhyme, first slowly and then with a beat.

10 minutes

Rhyming words

Children listen for words that sound alike at the ends of lines (feet, seat). Show more rhyming pairs on cards and ask children to say them aloud.

8 minutes

Making a new verse

Groups change the action words, for example “Jump, jump, jump up high …”. The teacher writes their ideas on the board.

10 minutes

Performance

Each group performs its verse. The class claps along.

6 minutes

Assessment

  • Participation, pronunciation and rhythm while saying the rhyme.
  • Whether the child identifies rhyming pairs.
  • Whether the child makes an action word fit the rhyme.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Children who are shy can do the actions while others say the words.
  • Children with limited movement can use hand and head actions.

Extension / homework

Teach the rhyme to someone at home and bring one new action word to class.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Sounds and Short “a” Words

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Word recognition

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Letter sounds and blending: cat, mat, hat, bat, rat

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children link letters to their sounds and use them to read and write simple words.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Say the first sound of a spoken word.
  • Blend sounds to read short “a” words such as cat, mat, hat, bat and rat.
  • Change the first letter to make a new word.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Letter cards: a, c, m, t, h, b, r
  • Picture cards: cat, mat, hat, bat, rat
  • Slates or mini whiteboards
  • Word cards

Prior knowledge

Children recognise most letters of the alphabet and know that letters stand for sounds.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Sound game

Play “I spy with my little eye, something that begins with /c/”. Children find objects in the room. Repeat with /m/, /h/, /b/.

6 minutes

Sound of “a”

Show the picture of an apple. Say /a/ together. Practise /a/ with the actions of opening the mouth wide.

8 minutes

Blending

Place c, a, t on the board. The teacher slides a finger under the letters and blends “c–a–t, cat”. Children repeat. Swap c for m, h, b, r and read each new word together.

12 minutes

Word hunt

Word cards are hidden around the room. Children find a card, read the word aloud and match it to a picture.

8 minutes

Writing

Children write three of the words on their slates and draw a small picture next to each.

6 minutes

Assessment

  • Can the child give the first sound of a word?
  • Can the child blend the sounds to read a word?
  • Check the words written on the slate.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Give extra practice with large letter cards and trace the letters in sand or on a tray for children who find letters hard.
  • Ask advanced children to find short “a” words in the classroom books.

Extension / homework

Find three things at home whose names have the /a/ sound and draw them.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

My School Bag

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Word recognition and vocabulary

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Classroom words, word wall and sentence frames

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children build a useful English vocabulary by seeing, saying, reading and using words in context.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Name and read at least eight words: bag, book, pen, pencil, ruler, eraser, box, bottle.
  • Match words with pictures or objects.
  • Use “I have a … in my bag.” to make sentences.

Teaching-learning materials

  • A school bag with real objects
  • Word cards and picture cards
  • Chart paper for a word wall
  • Sentence frame strips

Prior knowledge

Children know the names of these objects in their home language.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Show and tell

The teacher takes objects out of a bag one by one, names each in English and holds it up. Children repeat and say the name in their home language.

8 minutes

Word wall

The teacher writes each word on a card and the class sticks it on the word wall with its picture. Children read the words together.

8 minutes

Matching game

Groups match word cards with picture cards. They read each pair aloud to the group.

10 minutes

Sentence frames

The teacher models, “I have a pencil in my bag.” Children choose an object and complete the sentence orally and then on a strip.

9 minutes

Wrap-up

Play “What is missing?”: hide one object and ask children to name it.

5 minutes

Assessment

  • Number of words the child can read and say correctly.
  • Accuracy in matching words and pictures.
  • Ability to complete the sentence frame.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Add the home-language word to the word wall so children see both languages.
  • Let visually impaired children touch the real objects while learning the names.

Extension / homework

Draw your school bag and label five things inside it in English.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

My Pet Cat (Shared Reading)

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Reading

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Shared reading of a short text on a chart

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children understand that reading means making meaning, and begin to read simple connected text with the teacher and each other.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Predict what a text is about by looking at the picture and title.
  • Follow the text from left to right and top to bottom.
  • Answer simple questions about the text.

Teaching-learning materials

  • The text written in large letters on a chart or in a big book
  • A picture of a cat
  • Pointer stick

Prior knowledge

Children know common words like I, a, is, my, and can read some simple words.

Text (write on a chart)

I have a cat.

Her name is Mimi.

Mimi is soft and white.

She likes milk.

She likes to sleep in my bag.

Mimi is my friend.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Before reading

Show the cat picture and the title. Ask, “What do you think the story is about?” Let children share ideas in any language.

6 minutes

Shared reading

The teacher reads the text pointing to each word. Children join in on the second reading. On the third reading, the teacher pauses and children supply the missing words.

14 minutes

After reading

Ask: What is the cat’s name? What colour is Mimi? What does Mimi like? Where does she sleep? Do you have a pet? Children can answer with words, phrases or sentences.

12 minutes

Pair reading

Pairs take turns reading lines from the chart or from word strips. The teacher listens and helps.

8 minutes

Assessment

  • Does the child’s finger move with the words?
  • Number of words read correctly.
  • Responses to the questions.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children who are not yet reading say the line from memory while pointing.
  • Give a large-print copy to children with low vision.

Extension / homework

Read the chart to someone at home and draw your own pet or a pet you would like to have.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

Mina’s Kite (Reading with Understanding)

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Reading

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Guided reading, comprehension and giving an opinion

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children read a short passage with understanding and share what they think about it.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Read a short passage with growing fluency.
  • Answer questions that are directly stated in the text.
  • Answer “What do you think?” questions with their own opinion.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Passage on a chart or reading cards at three levels
  • Question cards
  • Picture of a kite

Prior knowledge

Children can read simple sentences and understand short stories told orally.

Passage

Mina has a red kite. It is windy today. Mina runs to the park with her brother. The kite goes up, up, up! Suddenly, the string breaks. The kite flies away. Mina is sad. Her brother says, “Do not cry. We can make a new kite.” Mina smiles.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Getting ready

Talk about kites and flying them. Ask children to guess what could go wrong while flying a kite.

5 minutes

Silent reading

Children read the passage silently. The teacher helps children who need support.

8 minutes

Group reading

Three groups read aloud by turns, each at their own level. The teacher notes words children skip, change or struggle with, and supports them.

10 minutes

Questions

Direct: What colour is Mina’s kite? Who goes to the park with Mina? Inference: Why is Mina sad? Opinion: What would you do if your kite flew away?

10 minutes

New words

Explain “string” and “breaks” with pictures and actions. Children use each word in a sentence.

4 minutes

Wrap-up

Each child says one thing they liked about the story.

3 minutes

Assessment

  • Note reading mistakes (miscues): skipped, added, changed or repeated words.
  • Quality of answers at the direct, inference and opinion levels.
  • Pausing at full stops.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Give slower readers a shorter part of the passage.
  • Ask fast readers to write one more sentence to continue the story.

Extension / homework

Read a short story at home and write one question about it.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

At the Park (Shared Writing)

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Writing

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Describing a picture in simple sentences

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children express their ideas in writing and begin to use basic conventions of English writing.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Write three or four meaningful sentences about a picture.
  • Begin sentences with a capital letter and end them with a full stop.
  • Leave spaces between words and read their writing to a friend.

Teaching-learning materials

  • A large picture of a park with children, a swing, a dog and trees
  • Sentence frames: “I can see a …” and “The … is …”
  • Word wall words
  • Notebooks

Prior knowledge

Children can write simple words and say sentences about a picture.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Picture talk

Show the picture of the park and ask, “What can you see?” Children say what they see in any language, and the teacher gives the English words.

6 minutes

Making sentences

The teacher picks a few ideas and models how to turn them into sentences using the frames. Children say the sentences aloud.

8 minutes

Shared writing

The teacher writes three sentences on the board with the children’s help. She points out the capital letter, spaces between words and the full stop.

10 minutes

Guided writing

Children write three or four sentences about the picture in their notebooks. They can use the word wall. The teacher moves around and helps.

12 minutes

Sharing

Children read their sentences to a partner. Two or three read to the class.

4 minutes

Assessment

  • Number of sentences and whether they make sense.
  • Use of capital letters, spaces and full stops.
  • For early writers, appreciate effort and ideas before correcting spelling.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children who find writing hard build sentences with word cards or say them to the teacher to write.
  • Ask advanced children to add what the people in the picture might say.

Extension / homework

Look at a place at home, such as the kitchen or the courtyard, and write three sentences about it.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

A Thank-You Note

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Writing

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Writing a short informal note

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Children write for a real purpose and audience and see that writing is a way to share ideas and feelings.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Identify the parts of a note: date, greeting, message and closing with name.
  • Write a thank-you note of four or five sentences to someone they know.
  • Check capital letters, full stops and spelling with a partner.

Teaching-learning materials

  • A sample note on a chart
  • Coloured pens or chalk
  • Note paper or notebooks

Prior knowledge

Children can write simple sentences and know what it means to say thank you.

Sample note

Date: ____________

Dear Aunt Rina,

Thank you for the colourful book. I read it with my sister. I liked the story of the little fish.

Your loving niece,

Anu

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Sample note

The teacher reads the sample note. Ask, “Have you ever said thank you to someone for a gift or help?”

8 minutes

Parts of a note

Children find the date, greeting, message and closing in the sample and mark each part with a different colour.

7 minutes

Shared writing

The class writes a thank-you note together to a school helper, such as the gardener or the cook. The teacher writes on the board and children suggest sentences.

8 minutes

Independent writing

Each child writes a thank-you note of four or five sentences to a friend, a family member or a teacher.

12 minutes

Check and finish

Partners swap notes and check: Is there a greeting? Is there a closing? Does each sentence start with a capital letter and end with a full stop? Children correct their own notes.

5 minutes

Assessment

  • Are all four parts present?
  • Does the message have four or five meaningful sentences?
  • Changes made after the partner check.

Inclusion and extra support

  • Provide sentence starters such as “Thank you for …” and “I liked …”.
  • Let advanced children add a question to their note.

Extension / homework

Copy your corrected note on clean paper and give it to the person you wrote it for.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Lesson plan 10

Assessment and Review (Three Stations)

Class

III (Class 3)

Subject

Language-II (English)

Language block

Assessment (speaking, reading, writing)

Duration

40 minutes

Topic

Combined check of listening and speaking, reading, vocabulary and writing, with self-correction

Date

Teacher

Goal / competency no.

(fill in from your textbook or curriculum)

Curricular goal

Understand each child’s progress in English in a playful way and plan the next steps for learning.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, children:

  • Speak three sentences about a picture.
  • Read a short passage and answer two questions.
  • Complete a worksheet and check their work with a partner.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Picture, passage card and worksheet for the three stations
  • Observation checklist
  • Star or smiley stickers

Prior knowledge

The practice from the previous nine lessons.

Teaching process

Stage

Teaching activity

Time

Instructions

Explain the three stations and divide the class into three groups. Tell the children this is not a test, just a way to see how they are learning. Each group visits every station in turn.

4 minutes

Station 1: Speaking

Each child looks at a picture and says three sentences. The teacher fills in the observation checklist.

8 minutes

Station 2: Reading

Each child reads a four-line passage they have seen before and answers two questions. The teacher notes skipped or changed words.

8 minutes

Station 3: Writing

Children complete the worksheet (sample below).

8 minutes

Self-correction

Partners check each other’s worksheets against a model answer. Children correct their own mistakes and give themselves a star or smiley.

8 minutes

Wrap-up

The teacher gives general feedback and tells the class what they will practise next.

4 minutes

Sample worksheet questions

A. Fill in the missing letter: c_t, h_t, b_g.

B. Match each word with its picture: pen, ruler, bag, book.

C. Put the words in order to make a sentence: bag / my / This / is

D. Write the sentence correctly: my name is ravi

E. Look at the picture of children playing in a park and write two sentences.

Assessment

  • Fill in the table below for each child in all four areas.
  • Choose the areas where most children are at “Still learning” and plan extra practice for them.

Area

Still learning (1)

Getting there (2)

Confident (3)

Listening and speaking

Uses single words or short phrases with support

Speaks 2–3 sentences on their own

Speaks 4–5 connected sentences with clear pronunciation

Reading

Recognises letters and a few familiar words

Reads simple sentences with some pauses

Reads a short passage smoothly and answers questions

Vocabulary and words

Matches a few words with pictures

Reads and uses most taught words

Reads, spells and uses taught words in sentences

Writing

Copies words or writes one sentence

Writes 2–3 sentences with some errors

Writes 3–4 meaningful sentences with capital letters and full stops

Inclusion and extra support

  • Allow children who find writing hard to answer orally.
  • Give all children enough time and look at each child’s own progress, not comparison with others.

Extension / homework

Tell someone at home one thing you did well and one thing you want to improve in English.

Reflection after the lesson (teacher fills in)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?
