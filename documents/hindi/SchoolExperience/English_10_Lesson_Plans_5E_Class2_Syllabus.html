How to use these plans

  • These 10 plans are written for Class 2 English and each follows the 5E model: Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate.
  • Lesson titles follow the contents page of the MP SCERT English textbook. The poems, rhymes and stories of the textbook are not copied here. Run the activities with your textbook text for each lesson and change the examples if needed.
  • Fill in the curriculum goal and competency number in each plan according to your textbook or curriculum.
  • Adjust timings to your period. In the observation list, tick the correct box for each child.
  • The supervisor will assess 5 lessons per subject. Keep all 10 plans in your file and fill the reflection after each lesson.

Lesson Plan 1 | Textbook lesson 1

Alphabet – a to d

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionWord Recognition (letter–sound link)Duration40 minutes
TopicLetters Aa, Bb, Cc, Dd: shape, sound and picture words
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children recognise and say the letters a, b, c, d (capital and small), link them with their sounds and name words that begin with them.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • say the names and sounds of a, b, c and d.
  • match capital and small letters (A–a, B–b, C–c, D–d).
  • name two or three words for each letter, e.g. apple, ball, cat, dog.
  • write the four letters in their notebooks with correct shape and direction.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Alphabet chart and picture cards (apple, ball, cat, dog)
  • Letter cards (capital and small)
  • Sand tray or slate and chalk
  • Textbook lesson 1 pages

Prior knowledge

Children have heard the English alphabet song and can name some objects around them in Hindi or English.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Sing the alphabet song with actions, pointing to each letter on the chart.
  • Ask, “Which letters did you hear first?” Show the picture of a ball and say /b/.
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Place picture cards on the floor. Children pick one picture, say its name and find the matching letter card.
  • In groups, children trace each letter in the air, in sand or on a slate.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher writes A a, B b, C c, D d on the board, saying the name, the sound and one word for each: A–a–apple, B–b–ball.
  • Show the starting direction of each letter. Children repeat the sound and copy the strokes in the air.
  • Point out that each letter has a capital and a small form.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Play “Find your partner”: half the class holds capital cards, half holds small cards, and they find each other.
  • Children write each letter in their notebooks and draw one picture next to it.
  • Pairs think of one more word for each letter and tell the class.
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Teacher says a sound, children hold up the correct letter card.
  • Show a picture and ask for its first letter. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Says the name and sound of a–d
Matches capital and small letters
Names a word for each letter
Writes the letters correctly

Inclusion and extra support

  • Give extra tracing practice on a dotted-letter sheet to children who find writing hard.
  • Allow children to say the Hindi name of the picture first, then guide them to the English word.
  • Ask quick learners to find objects in the classroom starting with a, b, c or d.

Extension / homework

Look at things at home and tell an elder one thing that starts with each of a, b, c and d.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson Plan 2 | Textbook lesson 4

Alphabet – m to p

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionWord Recognition (letter–sound link)Duration40 minutes
TopicLetters Mm, Nn, Oo, Pp: shape, sound and picture words
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children recognise and say the letters m, n, o, p, link them with sounds and use them in simple words.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • name and sound the letters m, n, o and p.
  • tell the first letter of words such as mango, nest, orange, parrot.
  • read and write simple words such as mat, nut, pot and pen with help.
  • tell capital and small letters apart.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Alphabet chart
  • Picture cards: mango, nest, orange, parrot
  • Letter cards m, n, o, p
  • Textbook lesson 4 pages

Prior knowledge

Children know letters a to l and some words that begin with them.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Revise a to l with the alphabet chart in a quick “point and say” round.
  • Show a mango and ask, “What is this? Which sound do you hear first?”
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Groups get a bag of picture cards and sort them under the letters m, n, o and p.
  • Children say each picture name slowly and listen for the first sound.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher writes M m, N n, O o, P p on the board with a sound and a picture word for each.
  • Teacher shows how to blend: m–a–t = mat, p–o–t = pot, n–u–t = nut. Children say the sounds and then the word.
  • Draw attention to the round shape of o and p.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Play “Letter hunt”: children look around the room and the picture cards for things that start with m, n, o or p.
  • Children write one word for each letter in their notebooks and draw its picture.
  • Pairs make a short sentence with one word, e.g. “I see a pot.”
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Teacher shows letter cards, children give the sound and a word.
  • Teacher says a word, children name the first letter. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Says the name and sound of m–p
Names the first letter of a word
Reads simple three-letter words
Writes the letters correctly

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use large letter cards and tracing with a finger for children who confuse o and p.
  • Pair hesitant speakers with a supportive partner.
  • Ask quick learners to make a new word by changing the first letter, e.g. pot, mot, not.

Extension / homework

Find two things at home whose names begin with m, n, o or p and tell them in class tomorrow.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson Plan 3 | Textbook lesson 6

Alphabet – u to z

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionWord Recognition (letter–sound link)Duration40 minutes
TopicLetters Uu to Zz and revision of the full alphabet
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children recognise the letters u, v, w, x, y, z, say their sounds and say the whole alphabet in order.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • name and sound the letters u, v, w, x, y and z.
  • say the alphabet in order from a to z.
  • name words such as umbrella, van, watch, box, yak and zebra.
  • arrange letter cards in alphabetical order.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Alphabet chart
  • Picture cards: umbrella, van, watch, fox, yak, zebra
  • Letter cards a to z
  • Textbook lesson 6 pages

Prior knowledge

Children know the letters a to t and can sing the alphabet song.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Sing the alphabet song to the end. Ask which letters come last.
  • Show an umbrella and a zebra and ask, “Do you know their first letters?”
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Show the six new letters on cards. Children match each picture to a letter card.
  • Groups put a set of letter cards in order on the floor, with the chart as help.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher introduces each letter with its sound and picture word: u–umbrella, v–van, w–watch, x–box (x sound at the end), y–yak, z–zebra.
  • Point out that x is often heard at the end of a word.
  • Whole class says the alphabet slowly while the teacher points on the chart.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Play “Missing letter”: the teacher removes one card from the line and children find it.
  • Each child writes the last six letters in a notebook and draws one picture.
  • Pairs recite the alphabet to each other with actions.
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Children say the alphabet from a to z, a few at a time.
  • Teacher holds up a letter and children say a word. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Says the sound of u–z
Says the alphabet in order
Gives a picture word
Orders letter cards

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children point to the chart while reciting.
  • Give extra time to children who mix up v and w, and show the mouth shape.
  • Ask quick learners to give two words for each letter.

Extension / homework

Sing the full alphabet song to someone at home and show them your favourite letter.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson Plan 4 | Textbook lesson 7

Bits of Paper

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionReading and ComprehensionDuration40 minutes
TopicReading and talking about the lesson “Bits of Paper”; cleanliness and caring for surroundings
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children listen to and read the lesson with help, understand it through pictures and questions and speak about keeping surroundings clean.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • listen to the lesson and point to pictures that match it.
  • read familiar words from the lesson (paper, bin, clean, pick).
  • answer simple questions about the lesson in short sentences.
  • tell one way to keep the classroom and school clean.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Textbook lesson 7 written on a chart
  • Word cards of key words from the lesson
  • A few clean paper scraps and a dustbin
  • Colours and notebooks

Prior knowledge

Children know that rubbish should be thrown in a bin and can name common objects in the classroom.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Scatter a few clean paper scraps on the floor. Ask, “What do you see? Is this good for our class?”
  • A child picks them up and puts them in the bin. Discuss why.
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Children look at the pictures of the lesson and tell what they think will happen.
  • In groups, they find and name objects in the pictures.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher reads the lesson aloud, pointing to each word. Children repeat after each line.
  • Discuss the lesson with simple questions: who, what, where?
  • Match key word cards with the words on the chart. Explain new words with pictures and actions.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Children act out picking up paper and using the bin while saying “Put it in the bin.”
  • Draw a clean school or class and write a word or short sentence under it.
  • Pairs make a class promise, e.g. “I will keep my class clean.”
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Ask questions about the lesson and look for short spoken answers.
  • Show a word card and ask children to read it. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Listens and responds to the lesson
Reads key words
Answers questions in short sentences
Tells a way to keep things clean

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let shy children show a picture or hold the bin rather than speak.
  • Allow answers in Hindi first, then help children say them in English.
  • Ask quick learners to say two more sentences about cleanliness.

Extension / homework

Pick up one piece of litter at home or on the way to school and put it in a bin. Tell us tomorrow.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson Plan 5 | Textbook lesson 8

Animals and their Babies

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionVocabulary and Oral LanguageDuration40 minutes
TopicNames of animals and their young ones: dog–puppy, cat–kitten, cow–calf, hen–chick
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children learn the names of animals and their young ones and use them in simple sentences.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • name common animals and their babies in English.
  • match each animal with its baby using pictures.
  • say sentences such as “A cow has a calf.”
  • read the animal and baby words from word cards.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Picture cards of animals and babies (dog–puppy, cat–kitten, cow–calf, hen–chick, goat–kid, duck–duckling)
  • Word cards
  • Textbook lesson 8 pages
  • Colours and notebooks

Prior knowledge

Children know animals around them and their sounds, and can use their Hindi names.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Make animal sounds and let children guess the animal.
  • Ask, “What do we call a baby dog?” and take answers in any language.
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Spread animal and baby picture cards on the floor. Groups match each animal with its baby.
  • Children tell each other what they know about the animals.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher names each pair: dog–puppy, cat–kitten, cow–calf, hen–chick, and children repeat. Stick them on the chart with word cards.
  • Teach the sentence frame: “A ___ has a ___.” Practise with two or three pairs.
  • Read the textbook lesson aloud with the chart.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Play “Find my baby”: children hold animal or baby cards and find their partner, then say the sentence.
  • Draw one animal with its baby and write both names.
  • Pairs say two sentences about their drawing.
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Show an animal picture and ask for its baby’s name.
  • Say a baby name and ask for the animal. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Names the animals
Names the baby of each animal
Uses the sentence frame
Reads the word cards

Inclusion and extra support

  • Use real toys or masks for children who learn better by touch.
  • Accept home-language names and add the English word beside them.
  • Ask quick learners to add more pairs, e.g. horse–foal, lion–cub.

Extension / homework

Look at an animal and its baby near your home and tell the class what you saw.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

8 minutes

12 minutes

(Extend / apply)

  • Each child draws one scene and says one sentence about it.

5. Evaluate

  • Children read word cards. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Retells in simple sentences

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let children retell in Hindi first and then guide them to English.
  • Ask quick learners to say what else the monkey could do.

Tell the story of the clever monkey to someone at home and tell us who listened.

Lesson Plan 7 | Textbook lesson 10

Hop a Little

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionRhymes: Listening, Singing and ActionDuration40 minutes
TopicAction rhyme “Hop a Little”: rhythm, action words and rhyming sounds
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children enjoy the rhyme, join in with actions and recognise action words and rhyming sounds.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • sing or say the rhyme with actions and rhythm.
  • name action words such as hop, jump, run and clap.
  • hear and say rhyming words from the rhyme.
  • say a sentence such as “I can hop.”

Teaching-learning materials

  • Rhyme from textbook lesson 10 written on a chart
  • Action picture cards (hop, jump, run, clap)
  • Open space in the class or ground
  • Colours

Prior knowledge

Children know hopping and jumping and may know other action rhymes.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Ask the children to hop on one spot. Ask, “What are you doing?” and say “hop”.
  • Say “hop, hop, hop” in rhythm and let children copy.
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Show action cards and let children do the action.
  • Children hop, jump, run and clap in groups while naming the action.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher recites the rhyme slowly with actions, pointing to each line on the chart. Children copy line by line.
  • Listen for the rhyming words and clap when you hear one.
  • Match action word cards with the actions and the chart.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Whole class says the rhyme with actions at normal speed. Then groups lead a verse.
  • Children make new lines by changing the action word, e.g. “Jump a little.”
  • Draw a hopping child or animal and write “I can hop.”
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Individuals say a line with the action.
  • Show an action card and ask for the word. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Says the rhyme with rhythm
Does the correct actions
Names action words
Spots rhyming words

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let a child with limited movement lead the clapping or sing the rhyme.
  • Check that the space is safe for all children while hopping.
  • Ask quick learners to add a new verse with their own action word.

Extension / homework

Teach this rhyme with actions to a younger brother, sister or friend.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

What went well?

Which children found it difficult?

What will I change next time?

Lesson Plan 8 | Textbook lesson 11

Will you play with me?

ClassSecond (Class 2)SubjectEnglish
Language sectionOral Language and ReadingDuration40 minutes
TopicMaking requests and asking to play: “Will you play with me?” and reading the lesson
Model5E (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)
DateTeacher
Goal / competency no.(Fill in according to your textbook or curriculum)

Curriculum goal

Children listen to and read the lesson, use polite requests and answers and speak about playing together.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, children:

  • ask politely, “Will you play with me?”
  • answer with “Yes, I will.” or “Sorry, I can’t.”
  • read key words from the lesson (play, ball, friend, me).
  • say one sentence about their favourite game.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Textbook lesson 11 written on a chart
  • Picture cards of games (ball, skipping rope, hide and seek)
  • Word cards
  • A ball

Prior knowledge

Children play games with friends and can name some games in their home language.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Roll a ball to a child and ask, “Will you play with me?” Let the child answer.
  • Ask, “What game do you like to play?”
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Children look at the lesson’s pictures and tell what the characters want to do.
  • In pairs, they practise asking and answering with a ball.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher reads the lesson aloud, pointing to the words. Children repeat the question and answer.
  • Teach the question–answer pair and polite words: please, thank you, sorry.
  • Match key word cards with the chart.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Role play in pairs: one child asks, “Will you play with me?”, the other answers.
  • Draw a favourite game and write a word or sentence, “I like to play ___.”
  • Groups perform their conversation for the class.
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Pairs show the question and answer.
  • Read word cards and say a sentence about a game. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Assessment (observation list)

Observation pointYesSomewhatNot yet
Asks the question politely
Gives a suitable answer
Reads key words
Speaks about a favourite game

Inclusion and extra support

  • Make sure every child is included in a pair or group; no child should be left out of the game.
  • Use gestures with words for children who find speaking hard.
  • Ask quick learners to add one more line, e.g. “Let’s play together.”

Extension / homework

Ask a friend or sibling at home, “Will you play with me?” and tell us what you played.

Reflection after the lesson (to be filled by the teacher)

Children listen to the story, understand the order and the message, and retell it in simple English.

  • listen to the story and answer questions.
  • read key words (dog, bread, water, bridge).
  • tell the order of events using the picture cards.
  • say in simple words what the story teaches.

Teaching-learning materials

  • Textbook lesson 14 written on a chart
  • Story picture cards in sequence
  • Dog mask or toy dog
  • Word cards

Prior knowledge

Children know dogs and bread and have heard animal stories in their home language.

Teaching process (5E)

StageTeaching activityTime
1. Engage
(Connect / arouse interest)
  • Show a dog mask and a piece of bread (or picture). Ask, “What does a dog like to eat?”
  • Ask, “What would you do if you found some food?”
5 minutes
2. Explore
(Discover / experience)
  • Show the story pictures out of order. Groups arrange them in the order they guess.
  • Children say what they see and make guesses about the story.
8 minutes
3. Explain
(Explain)
  • Teacher reads the lesson aloud with voice and gestures, pointing on the chart.
  • Ask: Who is in the story? What did the dog have? What happened at the end?
  • Correct the picture order with the story. Discuss what the story teaches in simple words. Teach key words with pictures.
12 minutes
4. Elaborate
(Extend / apply)
  • Groups act out the story using a mask.
  • Each child draws a scene and says one sentence about it.
  • Pairs retell the story with the picture cards.
10 minutes
5. Evaluate
(Assess)
  • Questions: What did the dog do? What can we learn?
  • Read word cards. Fill the observation list.
5 minutes

Inclusion and extra support

  • Let shy children hold the picture cards or play a non-speaking role.
  • Accept retelling in Hindi first and then guide children to English words.
  • Ask quick learners to say a different end for the story.

Extension / homework

Tell this story to someone at home and tell us what you learned from it.
